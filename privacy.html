Plain-language privacy

Your reading profile
stays yours.

ReadTune has no account, and the extension has no analytics or telemetry. Its core reading tools work locally. This page explains what it stores and when an optional feature can send text away from your device. The public website is covered separately at the bottom.

What stays on your device

ReadTune stores your reading profile, calibration history, per-page reading position and highlights, the dyslexia-friendly-menus preference, and the sites where you explicitly enable automatic behavior. These are saved in Chrome's local extension storage, on your device. There is no ReadTune account.

Reader View, "Restyle this page", and PDF mode read the text of the page or file you are on so they can reformat it. That processing happens on your device. The page text is not sent to ReadTune or anywhere else.

Read-aloud

Read-aloud uses Piper by default, a neural voice that runs entirely on your device. The default voice ships inside the extension, so it works offline with nothing to download. If you pick one of the other on-device voices in the Reading Lab, its model file is downloaded once from Hugging Face and cached on your device; the text you have read aloud is never uploaded.

If you choose the optional Premium voice, each sentence as it is read to you is sent to ReadTune's own text-to-speech relay (hosted alongside this website), which forwards it to a free third-party voice provider and returns the audio. Only the sentence being spoken — and, prepared a moment ahead, the one after it — is sent; not the whole page, and nothing is stored. If the relay is unavailable, read-aloud falls back to the on-device voice automatically. Premium voice, ElevenLabs, and cloud-routed Ask AI are the only features that send reading text off the device, and only while you choose them.

If instead you choose the optional ElevenLabs voice, the passage you ask to hear and your own ElevenLabs API key are sent directly from your browser to ElevenLabs to generate audio. Your key is stored only in Chrome's local storage on your device. ReadTune has no server in this path and never receives your key or your text.

Talk to type (dictation)

If you use the dictation feature, ReadTune turns on Chrome's built-in speech recognition. Chrome sends the audio from your microphone to Google's speech service to transcribe it — this is the browser's own engine, not ReadTune's. The transcribed text is placed into the field you are typing in. ReadTune does not record, store, or transmit the audio or the transcript itself.

AI reading help

Reader View has an optional "Ask AI" rail — it gives you key points for the article before you commit to reading it, and it answers freeform questions you type about the article. Where your browser already has Chrome's built-in AI ready, it runs there: nothing leaves your device for this feature.

Otherwise, the article text — and, when you ask a question, the question you typed — is sent to ReadTune's own AI relay (hosted alongside this website), which forwards it to a third-party AI model to generate the response. Along with the optional Premium voice and ElevenLabs voice, this is a feature that sends reading text off the device. A question you type can be more personal than the article itself, so send only what you're comfortable sharing. Where caching is set up, the relay may cache a generated summary or answer by the article's URL (an answer also by the question) — so a popular one is generated once, not once per reader — but keeps no record of who asked. An AI response can be wrong and is labelled as approximate.

The "Simplify" rewrite pill (for a passage you select) is on-device only for now — it does not use the relay above. If your browser doesn't already have a ready model, it says so rather than sending your selection anywhere.

Permissions

  • Active tab and scripting: used only when you invoke ReadTune on the current tab — to open Reader View, restyle the page in place, or start dictation. Access ends when you navigate away.
  • Local storage: keeps your settings and progress on your device.
  • Optional site access: requested only if you turn on automatic Reader View or restyle for a specific site.
  • Network access to ReadTune's own relay (readtune.tech/api): used only for "Ask AI" — the article summary and any question you type — when your browser has no on-device AI ready, and for the optional "Premium voice" for read-aloud, as described above.
  • Optional network access (huggingface.co, api.elevenlabs.io): requested only when you choose an extra on-device voice or add an ElevenLabs key, as described above.

What ReadTune does not do

The extension does not sell or share data, run analytics or telemetry, create an account, or send your browsing history to its creator. It does not use data for advertising, creditworthiness, or lending. The extension is open source and can be inspected on GitHub.

This website

The public marketing pages at readtune.tech are hosted on Vercel. ReadTune does not load a pageview analytics SDK, advertising pixel, or product-tracking script on these pages. As with ordinary web hosting, Vercel may process basic request and security logs needed to deliver the site; ReadTune does not use those logs to build reader profiles or connect website visits to extension activity.

Nothing you read inside the extension is visible to this website. Reader View, "Restyle this page", PDF mode, read-aloud, calibration, and highlights do not load website analytics.
