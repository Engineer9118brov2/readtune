Designing a quieter place for attention to land

The best reading interface does not ask the reader to adapt first. It adapts just enough to make the next sentence feel possible.

By Mira Chen   ·   7 min read   ·   October 14, 2026

Reading is not a single task. Some days call for a quick scan; other days ask for patience, memory, and a little more room around every line. A page that respects that difference gives attention somewhere steady to return.

That is why the strongest tools feel less like a collection of effects and more like a small instrument. They make the text clearer without making the reader wonder what changed.

Good accessibility is not a special mode. It is a better starting point.

The page should do less work

Shorter lines can make the eye's next jump easier to predict. Extra space can reduce the feeling that neighboring letters are crowding together. A sentence that stays visible while it is heard can give working memory a hand.

None of these choices is a promise for every reader. The useful question is smaller and more honest: did this change help this person stay with the idea?

Measure the moment, keep the choice

ReadTune treats the answer as something worth finding out. It tries a few arrangements, checks comprehension, asks how the reading felt, and carries the strongest combination forward.
