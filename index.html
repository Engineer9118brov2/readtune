01THE DIFFERENCE

Most reading tools give you toggles.
ReadTune gives you a starting point.

Fonts and spacing are personal. What feels helpful to one reader can be tiring for another. ReadTune makes that uncertainty useful: it tests one change at a time, checks whether you understood the passage, and keeps the settings that earn their place.

What's the main point of this article?

The article argues that reading tools should test settings on you, not hand you a pile of toggles — most of what helps is different person to person.

On by request, not by default

Ask the article
a real question.

A docked rail sits beside whatever you're reading — summarize it, ask it a question, or get key terms explained. It answers from the article first, and says plainly when the article doesn't cover something.

This is the one part of ReadTune that leaves your device: the article text and your typed question go to ReadTune's relay only when you ask, never automatically. Everything else — fonts, spacing, focus, calibration, on-device read-aloud — stays local. Read exactly what's sent →
