01 · Preference check

A small experiment.
Kept deliberately honest.

A four-minute preference check: one warm-up. Six short passages are scored. Two standard readings form the baseline; four other readings each change a single condition such as typeface, spacing, or leading-letter emphasis.

WBFSBFE
  • Scored order is shuffled to reduce position bias.
  • Speed is adjusted for practice effect.
  • Nothing wins unless it clears a minimum margin.
  • If nothing stands out, standard text can stay the answer.

Not a clinical assessment. One run is provisional. The point is to suggest a setup worth trying—not to prove a permanent “best” configuration.

09 · Evidence, not vibes

ReadTune does not call every feature “research-backed.”

Read-aloud and spacing have stronger support. Dyslexia-specific fonts, coloured overlays, and bionic emphasis are more mixed. The interface says so and keeps the weaker claims optional.
