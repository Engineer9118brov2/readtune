# User research — protocol & scripts

The award-readiness plan makes participant evidence the top priority: 5–8
short, consented sessions with adults who experience reading friction,
recruited **after** the Web Store launch from people who found the extension
on their own. This file is the ready-to-use kit.

The goal is not to turn the calibration into a clinical result. It is to show
that ReadTune is useful, respectful, and usable in a real reading routine —
and to make at least two product changes that trace directly to what people
said (logged in [`FEEDBACK.md`](FEEDBACK.md)).

---

## Who

- 5–8 adults who self-identify as dyslexic, ADHD, or as having persistent
  reading fatigue. Aim for at least two of those experiences represented.
- Recruited after launch, from people who discovered the published extension
  (a comment, a group post reply, a Web Store review). Not friends or family.
- Record a participant ID (P1, P2…), never a name, in notes.

## Consent (say this, get a yes on record)

> This is product feedback, not medical research. Nothing you say is stored by
> the extension. I'll take notes under a code, not your name. Recording is
> optional. You can skip anything, stop anytime, or ask me to delete a clip
> afterwards, with no consequence.

Get a **separate** explicit yes before using any quote or clip publicly.

## Recruiting message

> I built a free Chrome extension for people who find reading on screens slow
> or tiring. I'm looking for adults willing to try it on one real reading task
> for 20–30 minutes and tell me what's confusing or annoying. It's product
> feedback, not medical research — I'll pay [amount] for your time, recording
> is optional, and you can stop anytime.

## Session (20–30 min)

1. **Context (3 min).** What do you read that's hard? What have you tried —
   Speechify, Immersive Reader, nothing? What device? Where did you find
   ReadTune?
2. **First run, no help (10 min).** Ask them to install/open it and get to a
   point where an article feels better to read. Say as little as possible.
   Note every hesitation, wrong turn, and "wait, what does this do?"
3. **A real task (7 min).** Have them bring or pick a genuine article,
   assignment, or PDF and actually read part of it with ReadTune.
4. **Close (5 min).** Would you use this tomorrow? What would you remove?
   What's missing? One thing that surprised you? (If consented) one sentence
   in your words about what it did or didn't do.

## What to capture per session

- Discovery channel, device, reading task, prior tools
- Where they hesitated / what they picked / what they ignored
- Would-use-again (yes / no / maybe + why)
- Any error or dead end they hit
- One consented quote, if offered

## Turning it into evidence

- After ~3 sessions, look for a signal that repeats. Don't act on a one-off.
- Ship a change, note it in `FEEDBACK.md` as: signal → decision → change →
  (if you can re-test) the participant's reaction.
- Target: **≥ 2 shipped changes** traceable to repeated feedback by the
  submission deadline.

## What NOT to claim from this

- "Proven to improve comprehension" — usability sessions don't establish that.
- Numbers from calibration runs during sessions are not results; they're demo
  telemetry at best.
- Launch reviews are "early feedback," dated as such — not efficacy evidence.
