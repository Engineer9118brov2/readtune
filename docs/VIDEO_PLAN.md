# ReadTune participant video plan

Drafted on Monday, August 31, 2026.

## Goal

Make one short video that feels real, not staged:

- a dyslexic person uses ReadTune on an actual reading task
- the viewer understands the problem in under 10 seconds
- the product's differentiator is obvious: it tries the settings with you and suggests a starting point, instead of dumping toggles on the user
- we stay honest: this is not a diagnosis, not a cure, and not a claim that one setting works for everyone

## Format

Target length: 75 to 105 seconds.

Structure:

1. real person, real friction
2. quick glimpse of calibration
3. same task with ReadTune
4. their reaction in their own words
5. short end card with repo / install / "free and private"

## Non-negotiables

- Use a real consenting participant who self-identifies as dyslexic.
- Use an adult unless a parent/guardian and school-safe process are already in place.
- Pay them for their time.
- Let them stop, retry, or cut any clip they do not want used.
- Do not ask them to exaggerate difficulty.
- Do not write their reaction for them.
- Do not claim ReadTune "treats" or "fixes" dyslexia.
- If we cannot get a real participant, do not fake one. Use a founder demo plus a consented quote or voice note from a tester instead.

## What we need before filming

- A stable build with the pacing-default bug fixed.
- One article page that is dense but readable in both normal view and Reader View.
- One PDF worksheet or handout.
- A participant who has already had the product explained to them before cameras roll.
- A simple consent form that covers recording, editing, and posting.
- A calm room, good audio, and no time pressure.
- If using a premium voice, configure it before filming. Do not capture the key field, the key label, or any account-specific voice IDs on screen.

## Best story to film

Use one concrete task:

- "Read this article section and tell me the main point."

That works better than a generic product walkthrough because the viewer can instantly compare:

- before: dense page, effort visible
- after: calmer reading surface, clearer pacing, lower friction

## Shoot plan

### 1. Cold open — 0:00 to 0:08

Camera on participant.

Prompt:

- "Reading online gets tiring for me when the page is crowded."

Use their real words if they say something better.

### 2. Baseline friction — 0:08 to 0:20

Screen record a normal dense page.

Capture:

- scrolling
- crowding
- visual noise
- participant briefly explaining what feels hard

Do not make them fail on camera. We are showing friction, not humiliation.

### 3. Calibration glimpse — 0:20 to 0:35

Show only the fastest understandable parts:

- one passage
- one comprehension question
- one result card

Voiceover:

- "ReadTune changes one thing at a time and checks how it went for this reader — reading time, a comprehension question, and how it felt."

### 4. ReadTune in use — 0:35 to 1:00

Show the same or similar reading task in:

- Reader View for a clean article experience
- Restyle mode if keeping the original page layout is more convincing

Best visible wins to capture:

- calmer spacing
- font change if it genuinely helps them
- bionic anchors only if the participant actually likes them
- read-aloud fallback if they use it

### 5. Participant reaction — 1:00 to 1:15

Ask one prompt:

- "What feels different here?"

Good answers mention:

- less crowded
- easier to stay on the line
- less tiring
- easier to keep place

### 6. End card — 1:15 to 1:30

On screen:

- ReadTune
- Suggests a reading setup to try
- Free
- Private
- Works on articles, live pages, and PDFs

## Interview prompts

Ask these before or after the screen capture, not while they are actively reading:

- "What usually makes online reading tiring for you?"
- "What part of this version felt easier or calmer?"
- "Would you use this for school, work, or both?"
- "Was there anything that did not help?"

That last question matters. A small honest criticism makes the whole video more believable.

## Recording setup

- Screen capture at 1440p if possible.
- One face camera shot, framed chest-up.
- External mic or AirPods mic if a real mic is unavailable.
- Browser zoom and text large enough to read in the final edit.
- Mouse cursor visible and not too fast.
- Captions burned in or added as closed captions.

## Editing rules

- Open with the human, not the logo.
- Keep jump cuts tight.
- Use captions with high contrast and generous line spacing.
- Avoid flashy transitions, motion-heavy titles, or tiny annotation callouts.
- Keep one music bed at low volume or use no music.
- Leave in one natural pause or breath so it does not feel synthetic.

## Claims guardrail

Allowed:

- "It checks which settings seem to help this reader, and suggests a starting point instead of a wall of toggles."
- "Reading, the profile, and read-aloud all stay on the device. Dictation and the optional premium voice send data, and the app says so."

Not allowed:

- "It treats dyslexia."
- "It works for every dyslexic person."
- "This result is scientifically validated."

## Four-day sprint

Example timeline starting Monday, August 31, 2026:

1. Monday, August 31, 2026: recruit participant, send consent, pick article and PDF, lock build.
2. Tuesday, September 1, 2026: dry run the product, rehearse prompts, test screen/audio capture.
3. Wednesday, September 2, 2026: film participant and screen footage.
4. Thursday, September 3, 2026: cut the 90-second version, add captions, get participant approval on their clip.

## Success test

Before publishing, show the cut to one person who has never seen ReadTune and ask:

- "What problem is this solving?"
- "What is new about it?"
- "Did the person on screen feel real?"

If they cannot answer all three, the edit still needs work.
